TechMahindra are trying to build an AI-driven ticket routing and reassignment solution for Vodafone’s IT service environment, sitting on top of BMC Helix/BMC CMDB. The objective is to replace or reduce the manual work currently done by the global service desk, where Level 1 engineers read incident tickets, inspect service blueprints / configuration item mappings, and decide which resolver group should receive the ticket.
 
The problem they are solving is that tickets are often routed to the wrong resolver group first, then bounced back and forth between teams before reaching the correct owner. They want the new solution to understand the ticket context early, determine the most appropriate resolver group, and either assign the ticket correctly at creation time or re-route it directly to the right downstream team if the first assignment proves incorrect. 
 
They described this as more than simple automation. In their view, it requires an “intelligence build” that can interpret natural-language ticket descriptions, combine that with configuration and dependency data, and make a more accurate routing decision than past ML-only approaches. They also want the assignment to be largely autonomous, rather than just an advisory copilot for the service desk.
 
Their proposed delivery shape is a 4-week study / assessment, run in parallel with solution build, followed by an MVP for one business function, and then scale-out to additional functions if the MVP succeeds. They want to leverage Vodafone’s existing AI Booster foundation rather than introduce a brand-new platform, mainly to reduce approval friction and accelerate kick-off.
 
What they mean by the knowledge graph
The knowledge graph is central to their design. TechMahindra said previous model-based attempts achieved only around 65–70% accuracy, whereas the customer expectation is 95%+. Their explanation for the shortfall is that prior approaches were trained mostly on historical ticket behaviour, without a contextual knowledge layer that captures application dependencies, infrastructure relationships, ownership, and resolver-group mappings.
 
Specifically, they want a graph-like model that represents relationships such as: an application depends on servers, databases, storage, networks, and other assets; those assets are owned by different support teams; and those relationships should be available at ticket creation and reassignment time so the system can reason about where the incident should go. They explicitly described the need for a “knowledge graph kind of deployment” that understands the application and its dependencies on infrastructure assets such as server, database, storage, and ownership mappings.
 
They also linked the graph to a semantic model / contextualized view for the agents, so the agent can reason over entity relationships rather than rely only on past ticket patterns. Their stated intent was to extract structured data from BMC databases / CMDB into a Microsoft data layer and create the knowledge graph there to improve routing accuracy.
