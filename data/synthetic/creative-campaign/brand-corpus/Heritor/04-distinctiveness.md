# Heritor — distinctiveness benchmark

## Competitive set

- **Patek Philippe** — heritage maker, family-line storytelling.
- **A. Lange & Söhne** — German precision, movement-forward.
- **F.P. Journe** — independent watchmaker, austere modernity.
- **Vacheron Constantin** — Geneva heritage, lifestyle slips through.

## Where Heritor must remain distinct

| Code | Heritor owns | Avoid (read as competitor) |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | Ink + brass + parchment | Patek's white-on-blue gradient |
| Surface | Macro on bench, archive ground | Lifestyle on yacht (Vacheron) |
| Voice | Watchmaker named, calibre cited | Family-line storytelling (Patek) |
| Composition | Macro of movement | Wrist-on-cuff (F.P. Journe austere) |
| Heritage cue | 1875 + Geneva stamp | "Generations" copy (Patek) |
| Edition language | "Edition of 250" (specific) | "Limited" (vague — Vacheron) |

## Distinctiveness rubric

High when:

- Macro of the movement is visible.
- Watchmaker is named.
- Edition number is cited as a specific integer.
- Surface is bench, parchment, or archival ground.

Low when:

- Composition swaps to a wrist on a cuff and could be Patek.
- Lifestyle context (yacht, Monaco, ski lodge).
- Edition language is vague ("limited", "rare", "exclusive") rather
  than numbered.
