# Blueprint Microsite — Design

**Date:** 2026-05-03
**Owner:** Artur Zielinski
**Audience for the artefact:** internal Microsoft team (engineers, field, leadership). Used to walk through the blueprint thesis end-to-end and then run a working session on what the team should commit to.
**Sister artefacts:** [docs/blueprint-script.md](../../blueprint-script.md) (external one-pager prompt) · [docs/blueprint.md](../../blueprint.md) (the captured argument and copy)

---

## 1. Why this exists

The blueprint thesis already lives in two places: the editorial one-pager
([blueprint-script.md](../../blueprint-script.md)) and the captured argument
([blueprint.md](../../blueprint.md)). Both are static documents. Neither
proves the central claim — that we have an environment running, that the
parts compose, that the system extends itself — without a person in the room
to demonstrate it.

The microsite is the artefact that closes that gap. One URL. Survives being
forwarded with no covering note. Reads as an editorial essay until it
suddenly becomes a live, breathing thing that watches itself work.

It is *not* a slide deck, a marketing page, or a rebuild of the existing
Control Plane. It is a single-purpose page whose job is to make the thesis
unforgettable for an internal technical audience.

## 2. Audience and job

**Audience.** Microsoft internals — engineers, field, leadership. Mixed
technical depth, but everyone is presumed senior. They have heard the AI
transformation pitch many times. They are sceptical by default and they
will detect marketing register instantly.

**Job.**

1. Walk them through the thesis once, properly, in prose.
2. Show them the system as it actually exists today, drawn from the
   running codebase.
3. Show them the system as it actually behaves today, drawn from live
   telemetry.
4. Show them how the system extends itself — the meta-skill that authors
   new skills — and have an honest conversation about what's needed to
   harden that for production.
5. Leave the page open during a working session as the shared artefact
   for the team to argue with.

The artefact is not asking for buy-in. It is creating the conditions
under which an honest commitment becomes possible.

## 3. Aesthetic — single coherent register

**Dark editorial / observatory.** One register, not three. References:

- [linear.app](https://linear.app) — for the typographic discipline on dark
- [vercel.com](https://vercel.com) — for the editorial polish
- [radar.cloudflare.com](https://radar.cloudflare.com) — for the "live
  data presented as essay, not dashboard" feel
- [press.stripe.com/maintenance-part-one](https://press.stripe.com/maintenance-part-one)
  — for the prose-first authority (we invert to dark)

**Palette.**
- Background: warm near-black (~`#0a0a0c`).
- Body: warm off-white (~`#e9e7e3`).
- Mono / structural elements: muted slate (~`#9aa0a6`).
- Single accent: warm amber (~`#f4a300`) — same accent as the Gutenberg
  one-pager, deliberate visual continuity between the two artefacts.
- Validator-block / error pulses: a quiet brick red (~`#c54a3d`), used
  sparingly.

**Typography.**
- Headlines and pullquotes: a modern serif (Fraunces or GT Sectra).
- Body: a clean grotesque sans (Inter or Söhne).
- Live-system sections: a single mono (JetBrains Mono or Berkeley Mono).
  The mono is the *signal* that the section is connected to the running
  system — its presence carries meaning.

**Motion.**
- Restrained. No scroll-jacking. No parallax. No hero animations.
- The only meaningful motion is in section 5 (the live observatory): event
  pulses on arrival, line flares for ~600ms when a tool call fires,
  counters that tick.
- Section 6 is fully static.

## 4. Page structure — seven sections, one continuous scroll

Each section is one viewport-ish, generous whitespace, no abrupt breaks.
The page reads top-to-bottom as one argument. Sections 4–6 are the
demonstrative core; sections 1–3 and 7 are the editorial frame.

### Section 1 — Opening

Pure prose. The headline + subtitle + opening paragraph + pullquote from
the existing one-pager, transposed to dark. No imagery. No nav. Just
typography.

> *Why your AI hasn't compounded.*
> And the only thing we've found that does.
>
> If you've sponsored AI work in the last three years…
>
> *What you've been buying is a manuscript. What you need is a press.*

### Section 2 — The analogy

Full-bleed Gutenberg illustration, generated by Gemini (Nano Banana Pro 2),
warm-amber-tinted to live in the dark frame. Caption underneath. One short
paragraph reinforcing the unit-of-reuse-is-below-the-page point.

### Section 3 — The argument

Linear-Method-style stacked sections, prose only. Five short titled
passages:

1. **Skills.** Modular units of know-how, governed centrally.
2. **MCPs.** Federated capability layer over real systems.
3. **The harness.** Spin up on demand, tear down when done.
4. **Identity, security, governance.** Blessed once, not per project.
5. **The mechanism that compounds.** Why this isn't another "continuous
   learning" cliché.

Same body copy as `blueprint.md` §"The underlying argument, expanded".

### Section 4 — *The case of type* *(static composition map)*

Title: *What we have already cast.*

A horizontal arrangement, full-bleed, drawn live from the codebase.

**Three rows:**

1. **Skills row** — every `SKILL.md` under `api/server/skills/`, rendered
   as a small mono tag (e.g. `cv-crystalliser`, `rag-classifier`,
   `fleet-manager`). Grouped subtly by domain colour (a tiny coloured
   dot). Roughly 25 cards.
2. **MCPs row** — every tool in `api/server/mcp_tools/`. Same treatment.
   Roughly 15 cards.
3. **Domains row** — five or six wide cards. Real today: *Finance
   Compliance*, *Hiring*, *Onboarding*. Aspirational ghosts (faded):
   *Procurement*, *Legal*, *IT*.

**Lines** connect skills → tools (drawn from each skill's `allowed-tools`
frontmatter) and skills → domains (drawn from which domain's orchestrator
loads which skill). Lines faint by default.

**Interaction.** Hover a skill: the lines to its tools light up amber and
the domains that compose it brighten. Hover a domain: the exact subset of
skills and tools it composes lights up; everything else dims. Hover an
MCP tool: the skills that are allowed to call it light up.

**Numbers panel** in a corner: *N skills · M MCP tools · K domains
operating today*. Drawn live from the file count.

**Data source.** A FastAPI route, e.g. `GET /api/blueprint/composition`,
returns a JSON tree:

```jsonc
{
  "skills":   [{ "name": "cv-crystalliser", "allowed_tools": [...], "domains": [...] }, ...],
  "mcps":     [{ "name": "policy_search", "used_by_skills": [...] }, ...],
  "domains":  [{ "name": "Hiring", "skills": [...], "tools": [...], "status": "live" }, ...]
}
```

Built by reading skill frontmatter and MCP tool definitions at request
time. No build step, no staleness.

### Section 5 — *The observatory* *(live activity stream)*

Title: *And here is what is happening, right now.*

The composition map from section 4 is still visible, dimmed slightly into
the background. Events stream in over SSE.

**Event types and visual treatments.**

| Event | Visual |
|---|---|
| `agent.spawn` | Faint amber pulse on the skill card; mono line scrolls in: `09:42:11  agent.spawn  cv-crystalliser  HIRE-DEMO-04` |
| `mcp.call` | The line connecting skill → tool flares amber for ~600ms, then fades |
| `validator.block` | Brief brick-red flash on the connection; mono line in red |
| `workflow.suspended` (HITL) | Soft amber dimmed pulse on the domain card |
| `workflow.completed` | Solid amber glow on the domain card, fades over 2s |

**Counters** in the top corner: *agents alive · tool calls this minute ·
workflows in flight · exceptions this session*. Drawn from the existing
in-process `EventBus`.

**Data source.** A new SSE route on FastAPI, e.g.
`GET /api/blueprint/stream`, that subscribes to the existing `EventBus`
and forwards events with the minimum data the page needs (event type,
skill name, tool name, domain, workflow id, timestamp). Reuses the
existing `SSEHub` pattern.

**Behaviour when backend is down.** Section degrades to a *"observatory
offline — start the demo stack to see live activity"* message. Sections
1–4, 6, 7 still render perfectly.

### Section 6 — *How the type case extends itself*

Title: *How the type case extends itself.*

Static, structural diagram. No animation. The point is recognised, not
narrated.

**The two-tier story this section makes visible.**

There are two distinct authoring tiers, and the section makes both legible
without pretending they are the same thing.

1. **Design-time tier.** Where engagement work happens. The agent that
   sits with the customer (literally: a developer in their IDE running
   Copilot or an equivalent agentic harness) uses general-purpose skills
   — brainstorming, humanizer, writing-plans — to capture intent and
   produce a spec for the next runtime skill. These skills already
   exist; we use them every day to build the runtime skills below.
2. **Runtime tier.** Where the customer's environment lives. Two new
   runtime skills — `skill-author` and `mcp-author` — take a spec
   produced at design time and write the new `SKILL.md` or new MCP tool
   into the case of type. Governed the same way as every other runtime
   skill. Allow-listed to write to the skill catalog. Subject to the
   production checklist below.

Section 6 visualises **the runtime tier** in detail — it is the loop that
lives inside the customer's environment, and it is the part the team
needs to argue with. The design-time tier is named honestly in a sidebar
so nobody mistakes the page for claiming the runtime authors itself from
zero.

**What the diagram shows.**

1. Two skill cards, sitting visibly *inside* the same skills row from
   section 4. Same visual treatment, slightly highlighted:
   - `skill-author` — reads a spec, inventories the existing case of
     type, writes a new `SKILL.md`.
   - `mcp-author` — reads a spec, identifies missing capability, writes
     a new MCP tool.
2. Each card shows its `allowed-tools` list rendered plainly. Working
   set (to be reconciled when the skills are actually drafted in a
   separate session):
   - `skill-author`: `read_existing_skills`, `read_mcp_catalog`,
     `write_skill`, `propose_skill_amp`, `dry_run_policy`
   - `mcp-author`: `read_mcp_catalog`, `write_mcp_tool`,
     `propose_skill_amp`, `dry_run_policy`
3. Two arrows from each card looping back: one to the skills row, one
   to the MCPs row. The loop *is* the recursive point.
4. A small **sidebar** to the right of the diagram, in even smaller
   mono, naming the design-time tier honestly:

   > **Spec produced at design time.**
   > brainstorming · humanizer · writing-plans
   > Used by the agent that authors the spec. Lives outside the
   > customer environment. Already built; this is how we build runtime
   > skills today.

5. A short prose label beside the diagram, two or three sentences:
   *Skills are authored by humans, by other skills, or by both. The
   skills that do the authoring are themselves skills, governed the
   same way, with tool allow-lists that include the right to write new
   skill files. The spec they consume is produced one tier up, by the
   agent that talks to the customer.*

6. A small **"what's needed to run this in production"** panel beneath,
   in mono. Honest engineering checklist for the runtime tier:

   - Skill authoring permission gated by Entra Agent ID role
   - Generated skills go through CI gate before activation (APIOps)
   - Validator coverage required before any tool added to allow-list
   - Per-tenant skill catalog isolation

   This panel is the conversation-starter for the team. Not a sales
   claim. The bullets the team will recognise as the honest list.

**Note for implementation.** `skill-author` and `mcp-author` are not yet
shipped. They are designed in a separate session and added to the runtime
catalog later. For section 6 they are rendered as designed-but-not-yet-
shipped — visually present in the case of type, with a faint dashed
border distinguishing them from skills that are live today. The
production checklist makes the honest gap explicit.

### Section 7 — Closing

Quiet again. Two short paragraphs.

1. Honest inventory: real today, mocked today, aspirational. (Sentence-
   level, not a table — the table is essentially section 4 already.)
2. The closing line from the existing one-pager: *we don't hand you a
   repository and a statement of work. We hand you the environment,
   running.*

No call to action. No buttons. The page ends.

## 5. Architecture

```
web/blueprint/                          (new Vite app, port 5275)
  index.html
  src/
    main.tsx
    App.tsx
    sections/
      Opening.tsx
      Analogy.tsx
      Argument.tsx
      Composition.tsx          (section 4 — static map from /api/blueprint/composition)
      Observatory.tsx          (section 5 — SSE on /api/blueprint/stream)
      MetaSkill.tsx            (section 6 — static)
      Closing.tsx
    components/
      CompositionMap.tsx       (the SVG map; reused dimmed in section 5)
      ActivityFeed.tsx         (mono scrolling feed)
      Counter.tsx
    styles/
      tokens.css               (palette, type, spacing)
      reset.css

api/server/routes/
  blueprint.py                 (new — composition + stream)

api/server/services/
  blueprint_inventory.py       (new — reads SKILL.md frontmatter +
                                MCP tool registrations and returns the
                                composition tree)

scripts/
  generate_blueprint_image.py  (new — calls Gemini with the prompt from
                                blueprint-script.md and writes the
                                Gutenberg PNG to web/blueprint/public/)
```

**Why a new Vite app and not a route inside `web/client/`.**

1. The existing client carries the Control Plane visual language, which
   is a different register from the editorial dark we want here.
2. The blueprint page is forwardable as a standalone URL; it shouldn't
   pull the entire client bundle.
3. It can be started and stopped independently for the demo.
4. `web/portal/` is the precedent — separate Vite app for a different
   audience, same backend.

**Ports.**

- Vite blueprint preview: `5275`
- All data flows through existing FastAPI on `3101`.

**Boot.** Add a `make blueprint` target and a `npm run dev:blueprint`
script. Optional: have `boot-demo.sh` start it alongside everything else.

## 6. Backend changes — minimal

Two new endpoints on FastAPI. Both pure read.

### `GET /api/blueprint/composition`

Returns the static composition tree (skills, MCPs, domains, allowed-tool
edges, skill-loaded-by-domain edges). Built by:

1. Walking `api/server/skills/*/SKILL.md` and parsing frontmatter for
   `allowed-tools` and `model`.
2. Walking `api/server/mcp_tools/*.py` and reading the `@define_tool`
   registrations.
3. Walking `api/functions/workflows/*.py` and `api/functions/graphs/`
   to map skills → domains. (Or, simpler: a small static manifest
   `api/server/blueprint_domains.py` that hand-declares which domain
   uses which skills, kept under 30 lines and reviewed when domains
   change. Honest tradeoff: parsing the orchestrators properly is
   brittle; a hand-declared manifest is faster to ship and easier to
   keep correct.)

### `GET /api/blueprint/stream` (SSE)

Subscribes to the in-process `EventBus`, filters to the event types
section 5 cares about, and forwards a slim payload per event:

```jsonc
{ "type": "mcp.call",
  "skill": "cv-crystalliser",
  "tool": "ocr_extract",
  "domain": "Hiring",
  "workflow_id": "HIRE-DEMO-04",
  "ts": 1714738931.42 }
```

Reuses the existing `SSEHub` pattern; one subscriber per page open.

No other backend changes. No new MCP tools. No new skills.

## 7. The Gemini image script

`scripts/generate_blueprint_image.py`. Reads `GEMINI_API_KEY` from the
environment. Takes the Gutenberg prompt verbatim from
[blueprint-script.md](../../blueprint-script.md). Writes the resulting
PNG(s) to `web/blueprint/public/gutenberg.png`. Image is committed to
the repo (under 5MB) so the page renders without re-running the script.

The script is idempotent and parameterless. Re-run when the prompt
changes. Never logs the key. Never leaves the key in argv.

## 8. Phasing

Build incrementally. Each phase yields a complete, presentable page.
Stop at any point and the page still stands.

| Phase | What lands | Approx |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sections 1, 2, 3, 7 (essay shell + Gutenberg image). Vite app scaffolded. Image script written and run. | Half a day |
| 2 | Section 4 (static composition map). New `blueprint.py` route. Hover interactions. | One day |
| 3 | Section 6 (meta-skill diagram + production checklist). Static. | Half a day |
| 4 | Section 5 (live observatory). SSE route, event handlers, counters, pulses. Most of the visual polish lives here. | One to two days |

If section 5 ever proves too fragile to demo live, it can be replaced
with a recorded video loop and the page survives.

## 9. Open questions to be settled before each phase starts

**Phase 1.**
- Final font choices (Fraunces vs GT Sectra; Inter vs Söhne; JetBrains
  Mono vs Berkeley Mono). Decide on screen, not in spec.

**Phase 2.**
- Skills → domains mapping: parsed from orchestrators, or hand-declared
  manifest? Recommend manifest (see §6).
- What counts as a "domain" — the Durable orchestrator class, or the
  business name? Recommend business name to keep the surface readable.

**Phase 3.**
- Reconcile the working `allowed-tools` lists for `skill-author` and
  `mcp-author` once those skills are actually drafted in a separate
  session. For the page they are rendered as designed-but-not-yet-
  shipped (faint dashed border in the skills row).
- Final wording of the four production-readiness bullets.
- Sidebar wording for the design-time tier (brainstorming · humanizer ·
  writing-plans). Keep it short.

**Phase 4.**
- Which event types from `WAKE_TYPES` to surface, and which to hide so
  the observatory doesn't become noise.
- Demo script: which concurrent workflows to fire to make the page
  light up usefully without overwhelming it.

## 10. Out of scope

To keep us honest about what this is not.

- Not a replacement for the existing Control Plane.
- Not a customer-facing product surface. Internal pitch artefact only,
  for now.
- No authentication. Local demo on `localhost:5275`. If we ever publish
  it externally, that's a separate spec.
- No analytics, tracking, or A/B testing infrastructure.
- No CMS or editor for the prose. Copy is hard-coded; updates land
  through normal git commits.
- No mobile-first responsive design. It will look correct on a laptop
  screen used during a meeting; that is the whole audience.
